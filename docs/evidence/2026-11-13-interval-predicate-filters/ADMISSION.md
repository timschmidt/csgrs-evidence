## Measurement boundary

The matched late-stage experiment uses CBBC order, a common copied executable
path, CPU 0, disabled ASLR, a two-second CPU warmup and recorded frequency. Tracing
is measured separately, never inside the timed binary. No build or profiling
overlaps those timing runs. Each of nine cases runs both policies and three
methods; the matched comparison uses method zero, the public predicate. The
other methods preserve the original coordinate experiment. Four warmups precede
five batches of 32 complete predicate outcomes per mode. Assertions on exact
independent signs are included identically in timing. Retained mode excludes
source construction; rebuilt mode includes fresh fixture construction. These
are not cache-empty process measurements: setup, control checks and scalar
constants may warm caches. All successful results are materialized and consumed.

The initial coordinate experiment is a single exploratory run, not a matched
optimization claim. The native candidate's external 180-second containment
deadline is never reached and is not an internal total resource guarantee.
Original baseline and candidate executables are retained outside the archive
with hashes. final-artifacts and candidate-builds bind source/build options.
The external baseline lock was reconstructed after rejection and is explicitly
labeled as reconstructed, not an independently saved original snapshot.
Two trace-build-only unused-code warnings were fixed in the probe and the final
binaries rebuilt; the earlier log remains intact. No warning policy was weakened.

These nine predicate cases are sufficient to reject a placement that already
regresses its intended completed workload. They do not qualify a retained stage
for Hypertri, Hypercurve, Hypersolve or Hypermesh: the representative geometry
campaign, broader incidence/order/insphere families, and any future stage's
allocation/size/coverage and release gates remain open. No production optimization
is claimed on the basis of a scalar or predicate microbenchmark.
