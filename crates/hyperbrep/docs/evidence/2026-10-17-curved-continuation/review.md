# Ownership and proof review

The feature is full-size rationally rotated torus reconstruction followed by
native edits, further Booleans and cold archive validation. Its two missing
proofs belong in Hyperreal. HyperBRep keeps its existing strict validation and
Boolean authorities; no topology bypass, tolerance or approximate terminal is
added. Existing public APIs and archive schemas are unchanged.

## Certified integer boundaries

The three retained chart offsets are exactly zero, but their cold bounded
comparisons cannot certify the integer boundary. The independent rational
function oracle proves their numerator cancellations and bounds all fourteen
inverse domains away from zero. It treats pi and atan(3/4) as formal values;
the proof does not depend on a floating-point estimate or on Hyperreal's own
normalizer.

The initial normal-form-before-comparison experiment fails two regression
groups. Existing refinement exposes positive domain facts needed by the exact
certificate. The retained implementation therefore preserves the established
rational leaf and comparison paths and tries the shared rational normal form
only after the bounded comparisons remain unresolved. Unknown itself proves
nothing. Only a returned exact rational determines floor or ceiling.

The public controls use separately deserialized values for each query,
integer translations, signed 1/10^12 offsets and positive/negative half
boundaries. They also exercise rounding/truncation consumers. No precision
limit is increased. This adds a proof attempt and may increase cost; it is not
a performance-only change.

## Exact function congruence

The rounding repair completes the original two unions, but the swapped
workflow's second remainder initially fails cold validation at edge index 1.
The complete rejected archive and four endpoint coordinate pairs remain in
the record. Equivalent, differently written sine/cosine arguments prevent
the existing opaque-atom reducer from proving incidence.

The additional bounded quotient attempt admits function arguments using the
existing polynomial/quotient authority. Only the same function may reuse an
atom, and only when subtracting the two admitted argument quotients projects
to exact zero. Formal zero is sufficient for every value of the opaque atoms;
no algebraic independence is assumed. The argument expressions are admitted
before comparison, retaining inverse nonzero obligations. Nested arguments
share that attempt's atoms, memoized quotients and remaining budgets.

The attempt has the existing bounds: 512 visited nodes, depth 64, 32 atoms,
64 terms, degree 8, 4096 coefficient bits and 8192 charged operations. These
are per-attempt limits; the overall normal-form query now has one additional
attempt after its older successful routes decline. Existing matcher work,
allocation, whole-geometry growth and cancellation are not thereby globally
bounded. No failed result is cached; only a successful rational projection
publishes an exact sign. Local tests include unequal nearby arguments,
cross-function rejection, nested functions, valid and invalid inverse
arguments, low budgets and cancellation followed by retry.

The independent endpoint oracle checks all four captured pairs as exact
linear expressions in pi and atan(3/4), their function/coefficient identity,
and the native circle's endpoint formula. It is a proof for these pinned
fixtures, not a general trigonometric solver.

## Native acceptance

The original torus has major radius 5 and minor radius 2. Its 40-unit cutter
uses the exact rotation (3/5,4/5). Both union orders reconstruct the complete
torus, including from independently reloaded half operands. Each result then
undergoes two midpoint subdivision, reload, recut, remainder, reunion and
canonical-empty recovery cycles. Reload occurs before semantic queries.

Independent expectations are V=A=40*pi^2 for the whole, V=20*pi^2 and
A=20*pi^2+8*pi for each half. Fifteen points distinguish the cut, the central
hole, both radial boundaries and vertical extrema, including signed 1/10^12
offsets. Every checked edge has two oppositely directed uses. Full archive
checksums enforce a separate deterministic-output contract; they do not replace
these semantic assertions.

The remaining cone failures have a separate retained scalar identity involving
sqrt(5), pi and its reciprocal. Its independent proof and proposed next
experiment do not establish production support. No unrelated scalar debug
failure or broader release gate is claimed repaired by this increment.
