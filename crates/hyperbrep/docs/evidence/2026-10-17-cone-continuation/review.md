# Proof review and final scoped qualification

The original full-size cone workflows fail at exact chart and incidence
identities. Hyperreal owns these mathematical facts; HyperBRep retains its
existing topology, validation and Boolean algorithms. The final capability
claim still depends on full native and downstream acceptance. No scalar unit
test, diagnostic body count or archive checksum establishes set equality.

The new attempt follows every previous normal-form attempt. Its optional
relation state is absent or empty in those older attempts, preserving their
arithmetic and budgets. The existing quarter-turn atan quadrant calculation
is factored into `PiAtanLinearForm::quarter_turn_parts` with the same branch
logic, then shared by the new proof. The authoritative expression is not
rebuilt or approximated, and no archive schema or public API changes.

For a square root, the argument must first admit an exact rational quotient
projection with every inverse domain checked. Negative radicands decline.
Perfect rational roots use the existing exact `perfect_nth_root(2)` authority;
otherwise the attempt records the principal real root's square relation.
A repeated root atom can then be replaced by its admitted radicand. The
built-in Pi/InvPi pair has the separate certificate that pi is nonzero and
InvPi denotes its exact reciprocal. Neither rule assumes atom independence.

A trigonometric argument may project to `k*pi/2 +/- atan(q)`, with rational
q and no constant offset. The established quadrant selector determines the
signs and exchange of sine and cosine. A rational perfect root of `1+q*q`
certifies the positive hypotenuse; its reciprocal and q times that reciprocal
are the exact cosine and sine. Unrecognized forms, fractional quarter turns,
other atan coefficients, or irrational hypotenuses do not acquire guessed
rational values. Rational coefficient sizes are checked before root extraction.

For general admitted arguments, a sine and cosine pair receives a unit-circle
relation only after their argument difference projects to exact zero in the
same context. Rewriting `cos(a)^2` to `1-sin(a)^2` preserves value for every
valid real a. It reduces the number of cosine factors in each rewritten term,
while all term, degree, coefficient and charged-work limits still apply.
Numerators and already domain-admitted denominators may use these relations;
formal nonzero polynomials are not nonzero-domain witnesses.

Cold JSON repeats function expressions without preserving pointer identity.
The attempt may retain at most 32 completed rational function projections,
keyed by the existing exact structural equality authority. Each lookup is
charged and checks cancellation. A match transfers the already proved value
and argument domain, including through independently deserialized expressions.
Depth-zero arguments cannot bypass admission. Full caches stop retaining new
entries; neither failure nor partial work is inserted. Borrowed keys remain
owned by the input DAG, and the whole cache dies with the attempt.

The unchanged limits are 512 nodes, depth 64, 32 atoms, 64 terms, degree 8,
4096 stored coefficient bits and 8192 charged operations per attempt. The
additional attempt increases overall potential work. Existing structural
matcher work, all allocation costs, caller expression growth, and end-to-end
cancellation are not globally bounded by these limits. Coefficient products
can temporarily use twice the admitted bit count; checks precede multiplying
an incoming product by an additional root-square coefficient. No failed proof
becomes permanent negative evidence. Only a completed rational projection
publishes an exact sign.

## Retained circle incidence (candidate, not yet admitted)

The caller first retains the existing denominator-sign validation and proves
that the requested interval lies inside the unit chart. The optional shared
circle path then proves its support equation under the strict counterpart of
the caller's policy. A definite nonzero residual excludes the point. Zero
permits the existing homogeneous parameter inverse only for a quadratic or a
curve carrying its original quadratic frame. Distinct endpoints prove that
this quadratic chart is nonconstant: a degenerate projective frame lies on a
line, and a nonconstant rational image on that line cannot also lie on a
circle. They also exclude a collapsed affine source range. Higher-degree
circle support without a quadratic frame cannot use this argument.

The existing inverse keeps the root frame and source/local parameter mapping.
Its finite unit parameters are filtered by the original CurveParameterDomain2
with its exact endpoint authorities. No range or root identity is reconstructed
from approximate coordinates. Unresolved support, nonconstancy or inverse
proofs leave the old GCD authority available. Existing denominator validation
runs before even a definite exclusion, so poles cannot become successful empty
queries. The shortcut stores no new cache or archived proof privilege.

The original serialized native query fails with RealSign before this change.
The mathematically equivalent compact formula is already supported and remains
a control. Candidate tests include both queries, rational-circle parameter
oracles across reversal, subdivision and degree elevation, strict/approximate/
strict certainty, near-circle offsets, affine extrapolation, collapsed curves,
projective poles, and a degree-four twice-traversed circle chart. The full BREP
two-cycle workflows remain the capability boundary; passing these controls
alone does not establish their completion.
